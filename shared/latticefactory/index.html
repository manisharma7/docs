01

Executive Summary

Lattice Factory already wins the fence-extension pack within five kilometres of Banbury Road. Everything it is missing — Melbourne-wide privacy and garden screen visibility, the outer northern suburbs, the review count it deserves — traces to one cause: the business presents to Google as four names, two listings and two websites.

The assets are real. 557 ranking keywords, 182 of them on page one, worth roughly A$2,957 a month at Google Ads prices. A 71-review, 4.6-star listing that takes the #1 pack position for “fence extensions melbourne”, Preston and Thomastown, and a knowledge panel for “fence extensions reservoir”. A 73.6K-follower Instagram account. Forty years of trading history. None of that needs to be built.

What is broken is identity. The website is Lattice Factory. The Google listing is “Screening Solutions” by Lattice Factory — quotation marks included — with a primary category of Manufacturer. Instagram and the larger Facebook page are Screening Solutions. And since July 2026 a second Google listing, Timber Screens Melbourne, sits at the same address with the same phone, zero reviews and a different website — and it now outranks the main listing for “timber screens melbourne”. Google is splitting one entity's relevance and trust four ways, and rewarding the business only where proximity alone is enough to win. That is why the listing is in the pack for every fence-extension query within 5 km and in none of the Melbourne-wide privacy, garden or decorative screen packs — where competitors are winning on 4 and 12 reviews.

The good news is the arithmetic. Merging the listings, redirecting the second domain and settling on one name is about a week of work and unlocks roughly 70% of the potential this audit measures: the rubric re-scored with only those items fixed moves from B− to A−. The remaining 30% — a real Melbourne location page, suburb pages for the outer ring, schema, and a performance pass on a 7.8 MB homepage — is second-order work that compounds on top.

The Three Critical Issues

Fix these in week one, in this order. Each one is an entity problem, and everything else in this document compounds on top of their resolution.

Critical · Days 0–7

2.1 — A second Google listing at the same address is taking the pack position the main listing earned

Google Business Profile has two claimed listings for 41 Banbury Rd. Listing A carries 71 reviews, 97 photos and the full hours. Listing B, Timber Screens Melbourne, was created on 13 July 2026, has one Street View photo, no reviews, hours of Monday to Thursday only, and links to a separate domain whose own homepage says “WEBSITE CURRENTLY UNDER MAINTENANCE — PLEASE RE-DIRECT TO www.latticefactory.com.au”. For “timber screens melbourne” Listing B is pack #1 and Listing A is pack #2; for “privacy screens reservoir” they are #1 and #2 together. A name-match listing with zero reviews outranking the real one is what Google does when two records look like two businesses: it splits the relevance and lets the exact-match name win the query it matches. Every review, photo and post added to Listing A from here on is diluted by the same mechanism.

Reading the data

Three markets, three different prescriptions

Within 5 km, fence extensions are already won — defend them. Melbourne #1, Preston #1, Thomastown #1, Coburg #2, merbau screening Melbourne #2, and a knowledge panel in Reservoir. Seven of the eight pack placements come from one listing and one product family. The only threat here is the audit's own finding 2.1: Listing B is already taking pack slots in Reservoir and Melbourne, and a merge is what keeps this market.

Melbourne-wide privacy, garden and decorative screens are an eligibility problem, not a ranking one. The pack occupants for “privacy screens melbourne” have 4 and 12 reviews; Lattice Factory has 71 and is not in the top 20 of either the pack or the organic results. Nothing on the listing says privacy screens — the primary category is Manufacturer, there are no Products, and the page titled “Privacy Screens Melbourne” is not in the navigation. Fix the category, populate Products with the composite and merbau privacy range, put the page in the menu, and the review count does the rest. Decorative screens is the exception: QAQ, Metalworks and Yarra Outdoors are metal and laser-cut specialists, and the timber decorative range is not the same product. Leave it.

The outer ring — Bundoora, Greensborough, Epping — is a radius problem that suburb pages solve. At 6–9 km the listing drops out and installers with Bundoora and Heidelberg West addresses take the pack. The site still ranks #1 organically in Bundoora and #6 in Greensborough on the Melbourne category page, which proves the relevance is there. Marj Fencing and Joe's Fencing hold those SERPs with one page per suburb; six suburb pages with a delivery cost from Banbury Rd, an installed-job photo and a review naming the suburb is the 60-day version of the same play. Epping is the hardest: every pack occupant has an Epping address, and organic page one is all installers. Treat it as a Day-90 target, not a Day-30 one.

The fixes that matter

  1. Get the Seattle page out of the index. /contacts/ is a MaxBizz theme demo page — “Visit Us Daily · 411 University St, Seattle, USA · Call Us 24/7 +1-800-456-478-23” — and Google has indexed it under the title “Contacts - Lattice Factory”. It is the single worst NAP signal a local business can publish. Delete the page in WordPress and add a 301 to /contact-us/. Ten minutes.
  2. Stop the form embed from injecting a noindex tag. Every page loads a LeadConnector (GoHighLevel) form from a _preview URL, and that script writes <meta name="robots" content="noindex"> into the rendered DOM. It lands in the body rather than the head, which is the only reason Google is ignoring it today. Replace the preview embed with the published form's embed code from the GoHighLevel dashboard, then confirm in Search Console's URL Inspection (View crawled page → Rendered HTML) that no robots meta appears. One toggle in GHL; verify before the month is out.
  3. Fix the four titles over 60 characters and the three H1s with no place in them. Replace the Melbourne fence-extension title “Fence Extensions Melbourne | Enhance Your Outdoor Space | Lattice Factory” (73) with “Fence Extensions Melbourne — Pool-Compliant from $25 | Lattice Factory” (60). Replace “Privacy Screens Melbourne - Custom Outdoor Panels | Lattice Factory” (67) with “Privacy Screens Melbourne — Timber & Composite | Lattice Factory” (59). Replace the merbau title (62) with “Merbau Screening Melbourne — Batten & Flat | Lattice Factory” (57). H1s: homepage to “Timber Screens, Fence Extensions & Lattice — Made in Melbourne since 1986”; /privacy-screen/ to “Privacy Screens Melbourne — Fence-Mounted & Freestanding Panels”; /garden-screening/ to “Garden Screen Panels & Outdoor Screening — Melbourne Factory Direct”. All in Yoast and Elementor.
  4. Complete the schema layer. LocalBusiness is present and mostly right, which is more than most sites in this audit's category manage. It has an empty @id, a generic @type, no priceRange, no areaServed and a sameAs that omits the 12.4K Facebook page. Set @type to HomeGoodsStore, @id to https://latticefactory.com.au/#business, add priceRange: "$25–$450" and areaServed as the eight suburbs. Then add FAQPage to the fence-extension hub and /privacy-screen/ and BreadcrumbList site-wide — Yoast Premium emits breadcrumbs with one setting; the FAQ block is Yoast's FAQ Gutenberg block or a Rank Math schema. An afternoon.
  5. Cut the homepage from 7.8 MB to under 2 MB. LCP is 4,378 ms against a 2,500 ms target, and the cause is weight: 39 images, 70 scripts, 9 of them render-blocking, plus TikTok, Clarity, Trustindex, LeadConnector and a dentalking.ai tag. Convert hero images to WebP (Performance Lab or ShortPixel), lazy-load everything below the fold, defer non-critical scripts (WP Rocket or LiteSpeed Cache — the server is already LiteSpeed), and remove any tag nobody can name the purpose of. Target LCP under 2.5 s and Performance above 80. A day for a WordPress developer.

Location Pages

Melbourne is a product category, not a place. There is no page on the site whose heading says Reservoir, and no suburb pages at all — while the competitors winning Bundoora and Greensborough rank on exactly that page type.

Working

What's already good

  • /product-category/melbourne-store/ exists as a Melbourne-specific shop with six product categories and “Fence Extensions Melbourne” in the title
  • Suburb-level demand is proven: the listing already appears in 4 of 7 northern-suburb packs and the site ranks #1 organically in Bundoora and Preston
  • Reviews name the surfaces Google needs: “installation” (5), “fence extensions” (3), “screens” (3), “timber” (3) appear as review topics
  • Delivery and pickup logistics are documented (/delivery-costs/, driver texts 30–60 min ahead) — reusable as suburb-page content
  • Product-page inventory is deep: 60+ size-specific SKU pages that can be internally linked from a suburb page
Broken

What's holding them back

  • There is no page anywhere on the site whose title, H1 or first paragraph says “Reservoir”
  • Melbourne is a product category (/product-category/melbourne-store/), not a location page — no address, no map, no hours, no showroom photos, no directions
  • Two hubs for one product family: /products/fence-extensions/ and /product-category/melbourne-store/fence-extensions/ share the H1 “Fence Extensions”
  • No suburb pages for Preston, Thomastown, Coburg, Bundoora, Greensborough or Epping — Marj Fencing and Joe's Fencing rank in those suburbs on exactly that page type
  • Interstate pages (/fencing-screens-adelaide/, /wa-bl-carpentry-services/) rank in the 20s for Adelaide and Perth queries; the Sydney claim in three meta descriptions has no page at all
  • /contacts/ — a theme demo page with a Seattle address — is indexed and titled “Contacts - Lattice Factory”
  • No Service, FAQPage or BreadcrumbList schema on any page; the LocalBusiness block has an empty @id and a generic type

One Melbourne page, one Reservoir page, six suburb pages

The demand evidence is already on the site and in the grid. The listing appears in packs for Preston, Thomastown and Coburg and the site ranks organically in Bundoora (#1), Greensborough (#6) and Preston (#1) — with no page for any of them. Reviews name “installation” five times and “fence extensions” three times. The delivery page already prices delivery from Banbury Rd. Build /melbourne/ as a location page (address, map, hours, showroom photos, directions, links to the six Melbourne-store categories), /reservoir-showroom/ as the one page on the web whose H1 says Reservoir, and then one page each for Preston, Thomastown, Coburg, Bundoora, Greensborough and Epping with three things Google cannot get from a category page: an installed-job photo taken in that suburb, the delivery cost to it, and a review that mentions it. The interstate pages (/fencing-screens-adelaide/, /wa-bl-carpentry-services/) rank in the 20s and are a separate, later project; the three meta descriptions that promise Sydney have no page behind them and are doing nothing.

Strategic Notes

Eight observations that do not fit a table but change what the business does next.

  1. Reservoir is an asset, not a limitation. A factory address in an industrial pocket looks like a disadvantage next to installers with showrooms in Epping. It is the opposite: Google already grants a knowledge panel for “fence extensions reservoir”, the site ranks #1 there, and every northern-suburb pack the listing wins is won on proximity to Banbury Rd. “Made in Reservoir since 1986” is a more defensible claim than “Australia's largest range”, and it is the claim the suburb pages should lead with.
  2. Local packs are won on eligibility before they are won on reviews. Bayside Privacy Screens is in the Melbourne privacy pack with four reviews. Lattice Factory has 71 and is absent. The difference is not trust, it is category and content: Google has to believe the listing sells privacy screens in Melbourne before it weighs the stars. Category, Products, and a page in the navigation are the eligibility fixes; the reviews are already there.
  3. Pool compliance is a niche worth its own page. “pool fence extensions” carries a A$1.99 CPC — the highest of any product term in the audit — and the 2400 × 500 and 2400 × 600 flat extensions already state pool compliance in their meta descriptions. There is a /product-category/melbourne-store/pool-fencing/ category with no explanatory content. A page that quotes the Victorian 1200 mm barrier rule, shows the compliant sizes, and prices them wins that term and the Pool Owners Australia thread at the same time.
  4. The 73.6K Instagram audience is the biggest unused SEO asset in the business. It sits under the name Screening Solutions, links nowhere useful, and is not in the sameAs of the schema. Once the canonical name is settled, the profile bio should carry the address and the website, and every installed-job post should name the suburb. That is the review-and-photo pipeline the suburb pages need, and it already exists.
  5. The site needs an author, and it has one. Forty years of manufacturing lattice is the kind of first-hand expertise Google's quality guidelines and AI answers reward, and nowhere on the site is a person or a team credited with it. An /about-us/ page that names the people, the factory and the year, plus a byline on the guide and FAQ pages, turns the tenure claim from marketing copy into a citable source.
  6. The structural threat is not a competitor — it is Bunnings, and the lesson inverts. bunnings.com.au shares 474 of the site's 557 keywords at an average position of 8.6, and “fence extensions bunnings” (4,400/mo) and “bunnings fence toppers” (880/mo) are two of the site's largest striking-distance terms. The site cannot outrank Bunnings for Bunnings; it ranks for those queries because people searching them want an alternative. The comparison block in §8 — price per panel, timber grade, pool compliance, delivery — gives them one. Being the named alternative to the category leader is a better position than trying to be the leader.
  7. The category is contracting, which changes the strategy from acquisition to consolidation. With head terms down 15–24% year on year, the plan in §10 spends nothing on new categories and everything on collecting what the business has already earned under four names into one. That is also why the KPI targets are modest: a 13/17 pack rate and 6,500 visits a month are what consolidation delivers in a flat market. Growth beyond that comes from the suburb pages, in spring 2027.
  8. Scope limitation: Maps rankings were read from the local pack in Google Search, not from a Maps rank grid. The DataForSEO Maps and Local Finder endpoints were not available in this session, so §3 records pack presence (positions 1–3 in the search results pack, from each suburb) and organic position, not the full Maps ranking beyond the pack. “Not in pack” therefore means position 4 or worse, not position 20 or worse. The manual proxy: open Google Maps, search each of the 17 queries with the map centred on the suburb, and record the listing's position in the left-hand list. Backlinks were measured (710 links, 188 referring domains, spam score 35, only 51 from Australian sites) but not audited link by link; the manual check is site:latticefactory.com.au and intext:"lattice factory" -site:latticefactory.com.au.

Become one business before becoming a bigger one. Merge, redirect, rename — then build the Melbourne page, then the suburbs. Every step in that order pays for the next; every step out of order splits the credit again.
